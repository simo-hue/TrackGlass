Legal Privacy policy

Your touches stay between your devices.

Last updated: October 29, 2026

Trackglass is a free, open-source app that turns an iPad or iPhone into a trackpad for a Mac. This policy describes what the apps do with your data. The short version: they don't collect any.

What Trackglass does with touch input

When you use the trackpad surface, the iOS app sends your touch positions to the Mac you paired with, over your local network. The Mac app turns them into pointer movement, clicks, scrolling and gestures. Touch data is used only for that purpose and is never sent anywhere else. It is not stored, with one exception you control: the optional Record session diagnostic (Settings → Diagnostics on the phone, Settings → Advanced on the Mac) writes a recording of touch positions to a file on that device, only while you have turned it on, and only you decide whether to share it.

Encryption and pairing

Pairing happens once, by scanning a code shown on your Mac or by checking that both screens show the same six-digit code, and you confirm it by clicking Allow on the Mac. During pairing, both devices work out the same encryption key from a one-time exchange that the code authenticates; the key itself is never sent over the network. Each device stores it in its Keychain, every later connection is authenticated and encrypted with it, and it is deleted when you unpair.

What we do not collect

  • No accounts, sign-ins or personal information.
  • No analytics, usage statistics or advertising identifiers.
  • No crash-reporting SDKs. If you opted into Apple's app analytics sharing, Apple may share anonymized crash reports with the developer; that choice is yours and is managed in your device settings.
  • No network connections other than the encrypted link between your iPad/iPhone and your Mac, and (on the Mac only) a periodic check for app updates that downloads a small update feed from this project's GitHub repository. That check sends no information about you.

Permissions

On the iPad or iPhone, Trackglass asks for camera access only to scan the pairing code, and for local network access to find your Mac. On the Mac, Trackglass for Mac asks for Accessibility permission, which macOS requires for any app that moves the pointer or clicks on your behalf, and (on macOS 15 or later) for local network access, so your iPad or iPhone can find and reach it. Trackglass does not read what is on your screen and does not record what you type.

Children

Trackglass does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new date. Because the apps collect nothing, changes are expected to be rare.
